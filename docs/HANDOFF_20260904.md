whom, never stored. Reach is a four-state recorded decision, UNKNOWN schedules
nothing and gets reported. ONE permission function taking a purpose — because all
three reviewed designs shipped an escalation path that ignored the permission,

`build_ladder_escalation`). Seven named rungs ending definitively. SMS to

able to fire at all.

household, not about today.

**The caregiver check-in** (`cura/pulse/caregiver_checkin.py`). Words that had
been written, tested and imported for weeks without ever being spoken.

## Emergency services: decided

**Do not build a direct 911 rung.** Buildable in a day ($0.75/mo + $75/call,
self-serve) and wrong. It never fails closed — a stale address routes to the
wrong PSAP and delivers the wrong address, confidently, with no error at call
time. 47 USC 227(b)(1)(A)(i) bars artificial voice to 911 except for emergency
purposes and FCC 24-17 says TTS counts; a true positive fits, a false positive
has no statutory home, and zero case law exists either way. And NENA 56-501.1:
most agencies dispatch police at MID-LEVEL PRIORITY to silent or abandoned calls
— so a TTS call that does not survive classification gets a squad car, not an
ambulance.

None of seven medical-alert companies dials 911. All route through a human
central station. Cura already cannot dial 911 and it is tested.

**The last rung is `DISPATCH_LINE`** — the county's free 24/7 non-emergency
number — with a licensed monitoring centre above it. Dispatcher rungs get their
own script leading with "medical emergency" and "ambulance", address said three
times, because Wyoming has one selective router statewide on 1972 technology and
47 CFR 9.11(b)(3) excuses the carrier from delivering location at all.
